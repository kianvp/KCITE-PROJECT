■ The problem

A detector can be wrong and certain at the same time.

Fog, glare and motion blur don't always crash a perception model. More often the model keeps returning a clean, high-confidence answer — so nothing upstream ever triggers a warning. Those are silent perception failures, and they are exactly what AEGIS-PX is built to catch.

◆ How it works

Extract. Predict. Respond.

Three stages, and none of them ask the detector how sure it feels.

01 — EXTRACT

Read the activations

Tap feature maps from intermediate layers of the object detector while it runs. No retraining, no access to the detector's loss — just its internal state, exposed at inference time.

02 — PREDICT

Score the failure risk

A small secondary classifier turns those activations into a single likelihood that this frame is a failure. Lightweight enough to run alongside the main model.

03 — RESPOND

Do the right thing

A mode-aware response layer picks the fallback that fits the autonomy level: hand control back, or degrade safely on your own.

▲ Response layer

Two very different fallbacks.

What "safe" means depends entirely on whether a human is expected to be in the loop. AEGIS-PX implements both so the difference is concrete.

LEVEL 2–3

Driver in loop

Driver handback

Risk crosses the threshold → the system escalates to the human who is supposed to be paying attention.

  • ▸ Audible + haptic takeover request
  • ▸ Time-to-handover window with escalating urgency
  • ▸ Fallback to a controlled stop if nobody responds

LEVEL 4–5

No driver

Autonomous safe-degradation

Nobody is available to take over, so the vehicle reduces its own exposure until it can stop without creating a new hazard.

  • ▸ Speed reduction to a survivable envelope
  • ▸ Sensor reweighting away from the degraded camera
  • ▸ Minimal-risk stop at the road edge
